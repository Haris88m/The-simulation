# How the System Works

## One evidence baseline, many personal worlds

The platform maintains a versioned baseline derived from approved evidence. Each player receives a persistent branch that begins from that baseline but may contain different assumptions, interventions, and theories.

A personal world is not one predicted timeline. It is a probability distribution over many sampled trajectories.

```text
OBSERVED BASELINE
      │
      ├── Player A world → ensemble of futures
      ├── Player B world → ensemble of futures
      ├── Player C world → ensemble of futures
      └── Collaborative worlds
```

## What “one million worlds” means

A million worlds means one million sampled trajectories used to approximate uncertainty. It does not mean one million literal universes or one million players.

Different players reuse shared calculations until their assumptions diverge. Equivalent branches may share cached state, while every player's identity and action history remain separate.

## Player actions

A player may:

- observe the current branch;
- introduce an intervention;
- submit a hypothesis;
- add a fictional or metaphysical premise;
- challenge a model assumption;
- fork a world;
- import another branch's premise;
- request a consensual merge;
- create a timestamped forecast.

## Reality updates

Verified real-world evidence may update the shared baseline. A player's unsupported assertion cannot do so. Fantasy branches remain available even when they diverge from observed reality.

## The live ARG layer

The narrative is generated from authoritative state changes. A gate, echo, challenge, or cross-world encounter may activate when a branch materially diverges, a forecast fails, an unusual minority strategy performs well, or two player worlds become compatible.

The AI interface explains what the server calculated. It does not invent authoritative results.
